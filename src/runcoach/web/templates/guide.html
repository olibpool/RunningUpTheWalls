First steps

  1. Settings: connect Garmin, then set your fitness score (VDOT, from a recent race), runs per week and climbing sessions per week. When you create a plan you can enter a race time instead and the score is worked out for you.
  2. Life: add what you do every week, such as football (day, start time, how long you actually play, how hard it feels). Plans are built around it.
  3. Plan: pick a race (5K to marathon) and its date, or just get fitter (continuous improvement). Preview it, then save.

A plan that fails the safety checks cannot be saved. The preview names the rule it broke; the usual fix is a later date or fewer runs per week.

Day to day

Five tabs, one job each.

  • Today: what to do today and why, a one-tap "how are you?", quick logs for when life happens (drinks tonight, feeling ill, a niggle, going away), the coming days, and how the week fits together: your running, football and climbing as one bar of leg load, done and still to come, against a usual week.
  • Plan: the week, day by day, with paces and the reason each session sits where it does; and the whole plan, with its phases (base, build, peak, taper) and every sport's load week by week.
  • Progress: your streak, a square for every day you trained, load by sport, running distance, personal bests, paces and recovery. Add your maximum heart rate in Settings and Coachal also estimates your fitness from your easy runs.
  • Life: nights out, trips and weekly commitments, each with what it does to the plan.
  • Log: everything you did, week by week, matched to the plan.
  • Open a session to see its steps and why it's there. Mark it Done or Skip, or move it to tomorrow. A move that would put two hard days together is refused, with the reason. If you press Done and the watch syncs the same run later, it counts once.
  • Leg load is effort (1 to 10) times minutes, weighted by how much each sport tires your legs: football counts a little more than running, climbing very little. It's how the plan fits everything into one safe week.
  • How plans are built: the base block builds easy volume with short threshold runs and strides; build adds intervals; peak focuses on what your race needs. The last week is short easy runs and one sharpener, and the race itself appears on race day with the pace your fitness score predicts. Football counts as one of your hard days, so a football week has one fewer quality run.

How the plan adapts

Every change is logged with its reason on the session's page.

  • A missed session is marked skipped. Two or more misses in a week lighten the rest of it.
  • Illness or a niggle in a check-in downgrades hard sessions, then ramps you back at about 70% and 85%. With a fever or chest symptoms the first days are cleared completely: rest until the fever has gone.
  • No sudden long runs: no run is planned more than 10% longer than your longest run of the last 30 days.
  • A night out (Today or Life) keeps that day and the next easy. A holiday or trip clears those days and ramps you back afterwards.
  • Heat slows pace targets, and hard sessions turn easy on very hot days. This needs your location in the config.
  • Retest: when your runs suggest your fitness has changed, a banner offers a time trial. Your fitness score only changes when you submit a result in Settings.

Safety rules

Plans are checked for weekly load rising too fast, a long run taking too much of the week, hard days too close together, paces out of order, and a taper that exceeds the peak. A plan that breaks a rule is never saved. The limits are in config/defaults.toml.

Getting runs in

  • With Garmin connected, runs arrive by themselves at the next sync (see below).
  • Log, Upload a FIT or GPX file. It is matched to the closest planned session by day and duration. Anything it cannot match with confidence is listed for you to assign by hand.
  • You can also drop FIT or GPX files into the inbox folder; the next sync picks them up. The same run is never stored twice, even if it arrives from two places.

Garmin watch

Coachal talks to Garmin Connect, not to the watch: your watch syncs with Garmin Connect through the phone app as usual, and Coachal syncs with Garmin Connect by itself every 30 minutes and whenever you open the app. The chip next to the logo shows the last sync; tap it to sync now. When new data arrives, the page refreshes itself.

  • Set up: in Settings, Garmin Connect, enter your Garmin email and password, and the one-time code if Garmin sends one. Only Garmin's session token is kept, never your password. The first sync brings in the last six weeks. To send workouts to the watch, set Watch workouts to Push to Garmin Connect.
  • Runs in: new activities and their laps are pulled and matched to your sessions. Garmin files climbing and football as "other", so Coachal reads the activity's name: anything called Bouldering or Climb counts as climbing, and Football, Footy, Soccer or 5 a side counts as football. Garmin's daily resting heart rate and sleep fill in your check-ins (a value you typed yourself always wins).
  • Workouts out: the next 7 days of runs go to your Garmin calendar as structured workouts with warm-up, reps, recoveries and pace ranges. Start a run on the day and the watch offers the workout. When the plan changes, the workout is updated or removed at the next sync. Session pages show On your watch once pushed.
  • How it went: when you follow the workout on the watch, each step is recorded as a lap, and the session page shows every rep's pace against its target. If your last few quality sessions were all too fast, or all missed, a banner suggests a time trial. Your paces only change when you submit one.
  • Without Garmin Connect: every run session has a Download as a watch workout (.fit) link. Copy the file to the watch's GARMIN/NewFiles folder over USB.
  • This uses an unofficial Garmin library. If Garmin changes its login, the sync report shows a Garmin error while everything else keeps working.

Calendar

  • With your Google Calendar's private iCal address configured, busy times are respected and a session that a new event clashes with is moved. The address is a secret: keep it in config/local.toml or an environment variable, never in the repository.
  • Calendar events that look like a regular commitment appear on Life as proposals for you to confirm.
  • Subscribe a calendar app to /export/plan.ics to see your plan there.

Using it on your phone

Open the app in your phone's browser and choose Add to Home Screen. It then opens like an app, and the last week page you viewed still shows without a connection.

Troubleshooting

  • A plan will not save: read the rule listed. Try a later race date, fewer runs per week or a lower starting distance.
  • A form is refused behind a proxy or Tailscale: the proxy must forward the original host header.
  • Nothing seems to sync: the chip next to the logo says Sync issue when a step failed; tap it to see each step's result in Settings.
  • Garmin login failed: Garmin's session has expired. Connect again in Settings.
  • The continuous plan has few runs: it builds from your recorded load, so football and climbing as entered on Life count too. If Life says your watch records your games as shorter, take its suggestion. Running never drops below your recent running time.
  • No "How it went" table: the laps must line up with the workout's steps, so follow the workout on the watch and avoid pressing the lap button.
  • Health check: /healthz shows the app and database versions.
