First steps

  1. Settings, Athlete: set your VDOT (a fitness number taken from a recent race), runs per week and climbing sessions per week. When you create a plan you can enter a recent race time instead and the VDOT is worked out for you.
  2. Events: add fixed commitments such as football (weekday, start time, length, effort 1 to 10). Plans are built around them.
  3. Plan, Create a plan: pick a distance (5K, 10K, half, marathon), the race date and your recent weekly distance. Preview it, then save. With no race, choose continuous improvement.

A plan that fails the safety checks cannot be saved. The preview names the rule it broke; the usual fix is a later date or fewer runs per week.

Day to day

  • Home shows today, the next session and anything that needs attention.
  • Week lists each day's sessions with paces and the reason each was placed there. Prev and Next browse other weeks.
  • Open a session to see its steps. Mark it Done (optionally with effort and minutes) or Skip, or Move it to another day. A move that would break a safety rule is refused, with the reason.
  • Plan shows every week with its distance and block (base, build, peak, taper).
  • Check-in: log how you feel, any niggle, illness and resting heart rate. A raised resting heart rate may suggest an easy day, but nothing changes until you accept it.

How the plan adapts

Every change is logged with its reason on the session's page.

  • A missed session is marked skipped. Two or more misses in a week lighten the rest of it.
  • Illness or a niggle in a check-in downgrades hard sessions, then ramps you back at about 70% and 85%.
  • A drinks night (Events) eases the next day. A holiday or trip clears those days and ramps you back afterwards.
  • Heat slows pace targets, and hard sessions turn easy on very hot days. This needs your location in the config.
  • Retest: when your runs suggest your fitness has changed, a banner offers a time trial. Your VDOT only changes when you submit a result in Settings.

Safety rules

Plans are checked for weekly load rising too fast, a long run taking too much of the week, hard days too close together, paces out of order, and a taper that exceeds the peak. A plan that breaks a rule is never saved. The limits are in config/defaults.toml.

Getting runs in

  • Activities, Upload a GPX file. It is matched to the closest planned session by day and duration. Anything it cannot match with confidence is listed for you to assign by hand.
  • You can also drop GPX files into the inbox folder; the next sync picks them up. Uploading the same file twice does nothing.
  • FIT files are not supported yet.

Calendar and watch

  • With your Google Calendar's private iCal address configured, busy times are respected and a session that a new event clashes with is moved. The address is a secret: keep it in config/local.toml or an environment variable, never in the repository.
  • Calendar events that look like a regular commitment appear on Events as proposals for you to confirm.
  • Subscribe a calendar app to /export/plan.ics to see your plan there.
  • Sending workouts to a Garmin watch is not built yet.

Using it on your phone

Open the app in your phone's browser and choose Add to Home Screen. It then opens like an app, and the last week page you viewed still shows without a connection.

Troubleshooting

  • A plan will not save: read the rule listed. Try a later race date, fewer runs per week or a lower starting distance.
  • A form is refused behind a proxy or Tailscale: the proxy must forward the original host header.
  • Nothing seems to sync: Settings shows the last sync and each step's result. Use Run sync now.
  • Health check: /healthz shows the app and database versions.
